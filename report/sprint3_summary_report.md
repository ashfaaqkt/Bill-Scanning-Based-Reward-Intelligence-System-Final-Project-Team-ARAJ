## 2. What Sprint 3 actually did

Sprint 3 first established whether the Sprint 2 score was real — it was not, for
three separate reasons — and then found the one change that genuinely raised it.

| Fault | Effect | Fix |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 23 PDF receipts unopenable by PIL, 19 of them genuine | ~⅓ of the minority class missing from training | `dataset/rasterize_pdfs.py` |
| 22 source receipts present as both genuine and tampered | model could recognise the receipt, not the tampering | grouped cross-validation |
| Tampered images re-saved at JPEG quality 95 | quantization table alone scored **AUC 0.690** with no image content | `dataset/normalize_images.py` |

After all three corrections the quantization probe drops to **0.460** — chance —
and the model's own score settled at 0.752.

**Then one change recovered more than the corrections cost.** Receipts are
~1200×1600 but the model saw 224×224, a 7.1× downscale that reduces an overwritten
digit to ~6 pixels. Training at **448×448** raised pooled AUC **0.752 → 0.805** and
the real-receipt subset **0.781 → 0.864**. Verified by paired bootstrap clustered
by source receipt: p = 0.020 overall, p = 0.016 on real receipts.

**Cost of honesty:** the headline moved from an unverifiable 0.76 to a
reproducible 0.805. Every number regenerates from six commands.

## 3. Was the CNN the wrong approach?

Tested directly. `ml-service/forensics.py` implements 31 hand-designed forensic
features — Error Level Analysis, noise-residual consistency, JPEG grid alignment,
copy-move block matching, local brightness and sharpness, colour saturation —
each targeting a specific tamper mode produced by `generate_tampered.py`.

| Approach | Pooled OOF AUC |
|:---|---:|
| Forensic features (31) | 0.736 |
| CNN at 224 × 224 | 0.752 |
| Fusion (CNN 448 + forensics) | 0.790 |
| **CNN at 448 × 448** | **0.805** |

Forensics land below the CNN, and fusing them *lowers* the result (0.805 → 0.790;
ρ = 0.638). **Fusion is rejected — a negative result, reported as one.** That two
unrelated methods plateau here, and that combining them does not help, is the
strongest available evidence that the remaining limitation is the dataset.

## 4. Honest limitations

1. **Dataset size.** 65 genuine receipts, from 103 source receipts. Best epoch was
   4–14 of 15 — the model still saturates before the budget and then overfits.
2. **Synthetic tampering.** 100 of 129 tampered images were generated by our own
   script. Detecting them is a weaker claim than detecting real fraud. The **0.864**
   control AUC, measured only on the 94 real photographs where genuine and
   human-labelled tampered receipts share a camera, is the honest estimate of
   real-world performance — and at 0.864 it is close to the 0.90 target.
3. **Sprint 2's 0.76 is unverified.** The delivered checkpoint is EfficientNet-B0,
   not the claimed MobileNetV2, and scores 0.555. See the appendix of
   `fraud_test_report.md`.

**Reaching 0.90 needs data, not modelling:** roughly 500–1000 genuine receipts plus
independently tampered counterparts, ideally produced by different people using
different tools.

## 5. Anomaly detection — now live

`/ml/anomaly` previously returned a fixed 0.05 for every transaction. It is now
backed by a trained **Isolation Forest** (`ml-service/train_anomaly.py`).

**What it honestly claims.** The route is specified per-user, but a per-user model
cannot be trained — `firestore_receipts.csv` holds **19 real transactions across 3
users**. So the trained component is population-level, and `anomaly.py` swaps in
the user's own median reference as soon as they have 5+ receipts. Amounts are
compared as a **ratio to a reference**, never absolutely, which is what lets a
model trained on Indonesian and Malaysian receipts serve Indian ones.

| Metric | Value |
|:---|---:|
| False-positive rate, held-out real receipts (n=441) | **13.2%** — target < 15% ✅ |
| Recall vs injected synthetic outliers @ 10x typical and above | **100%** |
| Recall @ 5x | 0% |
| Training transactions | 1,321 of 1,762 |

**Read those two numbers together.** The false-positive rate is *set* by the
`contamination` parameter, not discovered — clearing the 15% target is by
construction and is reported as such. Recall is measured against **injected
synthetic** outliers, declared as synthetic. Neither means anything alone: a model
that flags nothing scores a perfect 0% FPR.

**Honest limitations.** The detector is effectively a threshold on
amount-relative-to-typical — with one informative feature an Isolation Forest
reduces to a percentile cut, and a feature sweep showed category and date columns
actively *hurt* (39% vs 100% recall at 10x). It cannot detect a receipt inflated
by less than ~5x. The check is one-sided: only amounts above the reference are
flagged, since inflating a receipt is the fraud, not shrinking one.

Ranjeet's `anomaly_detector.joblib` is **not** used — it is an IsolationForest with
`n_features_in_=1280`, the EfficientNet-B0 embedding width, so it is an
*image-novelty* detector rather than a spending model. `anomaly.py` rejects any
artefact whose feature count does not match.

Learning type, for the record: the tamper CNN and the forensic classifier are
**supervised**; both Isolation Forests are **unsupervised**; perceptual-hash
duplicate detection is **not machine learning at all** — it is a deterministic
algorithm.
